Data Preparation

Module 2 — Filtering and Cleaning the Career Market Panel

Project Scope

This page documents how the raw CareerCompass extract was reduced to the analytical panel used throughout the rest of the site. The panel covers one industry and one career pathway, as the project scope requires.

Element Selection
Industry Computer Systems Design and Related Services
NAICS 2022 code 5415
Career pathway Analytics roles: data analyst, data scientist, data engineer, business intelligence, business analyst
Date window 2026-03-25 to 2026-06-29
Final panel size 287 postings

Note on the industry change

This project was originally scoped to Pharmaceutical and Medicine Manufacturing (NAICS 3254). We abandoned that scope in Module 2 after a volume check returned only 39 postings across the entire extract, falling to between 2 and 5 once analytics roles were isolated. A sample that small cannot support salary, geographic, or skill analysis.

We checked every manufacturing code before moving on. The sector as a whole held 313 analytics postings, and no single four-digit manufacturing code exceeded 57. We selected NAICS 5415 because it is a standard four-digit code, it appears in the assignment’s own list of relevant industries, and it carries enough volume to support the required analysis.

Data Source and Connection

The source is the MET CareerCompass 2026 job-postings extract, supplied as 17 Parquet partitions holding 165,386 postings and 115 variables.

The raw files are not committed to this repository. Each team member downloads them from the course-provided folder and points a local path at the download location, as described in the README. The processed panel produced by this page is committed, so the rest of the site renders without access to the raw extract.

Filtering and cleaning run in PySpark through scripts/build_panel.py, which imports two helper modules, scripts/clean_location.py and scripts/clean_salary.py. The script writes one output file, data/processed/career_market_panel.csv.

Industry Filter

We filtered on NAICS_2022_4, the four-digit 2022 NAICS code attached to each posting. The extract also carries two-, three-, five-, and six-digit variants. We used the four-digit field because the assignment specifies four- or six-digit codes, and because NAICS_2022_4_NAME is blank throughout the extract while NAICS_2022_6_NAME is populated and supplies the industry label.

Career-Pathway Filter

Filtering on the word “analyst” alone was not workable. Within NAICS 5415 it returned 621 postings, but the results included security analysts, QA analysts, trade compliance analysts, and IT support analysts. “Analyst” functions as a generic corporate suffix, and including those roles would distort both the skill analysis and the salary distribution.

We therefore defined the role family positively, matching titles that name an analytics role directly rather than excluding unwanted ones after the fact.

Included patterns: data analyst, data scien, business intelligence, bi analyst, data engineer, analytics engineer, business analyst, reporting analyst

Excluded patterns: security, qa, quality assurance, support, trade, exploitation, acceptance testing, network

The exclusion rule removed only two further postings, which confirms the positive definition was doing the work. We kept it in place as a documented check.

Record Counts

Date Window

Every posting in the panel carries a valid POSTED date, so no records were lost at this stage.

Earliest posting: 2026-03-25
Latest posting:   2026-06-29
Span:             96 days

The extract covers a single quarter. Rather than impose a narrower window on an already narrow range, we kept the full span and recorded it. Any seasonal pattern in analytics hiring cannot be observed in three months of data, and we note this as a limitation rather than attempting to correct for it.

Cleaning Decisions

Duplicate Records

All 287 postings carry a unique ID, so the panel contains no exact duplicates. The DUPLICATES field supplied with the extract is blank on every row and provided no help.

Grouping by title, employer, and posted date does surface repeats. Inspection showed these are multi-location postings rather than duplicates: Esri, for example, advertised the same Enterprise Data Engineer role separately for California, Missouri, and Virginia, each with its own record ID. We kept these, since a single opening advertised in three states is three opportunities from a job seeker’s perspective. The effect is that geographic counts are slightly inflated relative to the number of distinct openings, and we note that where geography is discussed.

Salary

Salary required the most cleaning. SALARY_FROM and SALARY_TO are stored as strings and are blank on most rows.

Three rules were applied:

  • Rows carrying 0.00 were treated as missing rather than as a salary of zero. Four postings had this pattern, all of them cases where the source held a structured MonetaryAmount object the provider could not flatten.
  • One posting listed an annual salary of $401, which is implausible. We set a floor of $20,000 and excluded it.
  • Four postings quoted hourly rates. These were converted to annual equivalents at 2,080 hours so that all salary figures sit on one scale.

We report the midpoint of the advertised range. Where only a lower bound exists, the lower bound is used. Coverage is 39 percent of the panel, which is normal for job-posting data, and salary findings should be read as describing the postings that disclose pay rather than the market as a whole.

Experience Requirements

MIN_YEARS_EXPERIENCE and MAX_YEARS_EXPERIENCE are blank on all 165,386 rows of the extract, not only within our filtered panel. No experience analysis is possible from this field.

We substitute the education distribution where an experience requirement would otherwise be reported, and flag the gap in the limitations section.

Education Requirements

Six education fields are populated on every posting. We examined both the minimum and maximum fields and use the maximum.

MIN_EDULEVELS_NAME records “Associate’s degree” on 235 of 287 postings, which reads as a modeled floor rather than a stated employer requirement. Charting it would produce a single dominant category carrying no information.

Skill Fields

The extract carries four skill families. Coverage within the panel is uneven:

  • SKILLS_NAME: 278 of 287 postings
  • SPECIALIZED_SKILLS_NAME: 277 of 287
  • COMMON_SKILLS_NAME: 82 of 287
  • SOFTWARE_SKILLS_NAME: present on 278 rows but containing an empty array in every case

A more serious problem affects all of them. Across the full extract of 165,386 postings there are 2,025 distinct values in SKILLS_NAME, and not one begins with a letter between D and Z. The same holds for SPECIALIZED_SKILLS_NAME, which carries 1,834 distinct values under the same constraint.

The practical consequence is that no posting in the dataset lists Python, SQL, R, Tableau, Power BI, Spark, or any other tool whose name falls in that range. The skill lists appear to have been alphabetically truncated before delivery.

We report skill findings from what is present and flag the truncation prominently wherever skills are discussed. This limitation affects the skill-gap analysis in Step 3 directly, and we have raised it with the instructor.

Remote and Onsite Indicators

REMOTE_TYPE_NAME classifies 142 of 287 postings. The remaining 145 are recorded as “Unknown”.

We verified that Unknown means unclassified rather than misclassified: every posting whose LOCATION text mentions remote work is already flagged Remote or Hybrid, so no records needed recovery. Unknown is reported as its own category and is not treated as onsite.

Location Fields

The location fields in the extract are unreliable. STATE_NAME holds a valid state name on only about 65 of 287 postings; the rest contain “United States”, “Remote”, city fragments such as “Francisco” and “Austin”, or blanks. The structured alternatives are sparser still, with STATE populated on 92 postings, COUNTY_NAME on 39, and MSA_NAME on 16.

LOCATION is the only location field populated on every row, but it is free text with no consistent structure.

We built a derived state column using a four-stage rule, implemented in scripts/clean_location.py:

  1. Match a full state name inside LOCATION
  2. Otherwise match a two-letter state code between delimiters
  3. Otherwise fall back to the STATE column
  4. Otherwise map a small set of bare city names to their states

Two-letter codes recovered at any stage are normalized to full state names so that “CA” and “California” do not appear as separate categories.

Postings with a recovered state: 205 of 287 (71%)
Distinct states represented:     29

The 82 remaining postings are genuinely locationless: their LOCATION reads “United States”, “Remote”, “US”, or “USA”. These are reported as Unspecified rather than dropped, since a nationwide or remote posting is a real category rather than missing data.

Industry and Occupation Fields

NAICS_2022_4_NAME is blank throughout the extract, so the industry label is taken from NAICS_2022_6_NAME, which reads “Computer Systems Design and Related Services” on all 287 rows.

For occupation we use ONET_NAME, a controlled vocabulary, in preference to raw job titles. Titles are free text, so “Senior Data Analyst” and “Data Analyst - Senior” would otherwise count as separate roles.

ONET misclassified eight postings, placing them under “Models”, “Actors”, or “Information Security Analysts”. Inspection confirmed all eight are genuine analytics roles, including Advanced Data Analyst, Business Intelligence Developer, and People Data Analyst. We kept them and grouped them as “Other”, since our title filter rather than ONET defines membership in the role family.

Inconsistent Labels

Employer names are not standardized. Three patterns appear:

  • The same company under different spellings, such as “Turnitin” and “Turnitinllc”, and “Anduril Industries” and “Andurilindustries”
  • Applicant tracking system URLs recorded as the employer, such as “Jobs.baesystems.com” and “Careers.pnc.com”
  • Job titles differing only in spacing, such as “Technical Consultant- Enterprise Data Engineer” and “Technical Consultant - Enterprise Data Engineer”

We left employer names as supplied rather than merging them, since any merge rule risks combining genuinely distinct entities. Employer counts should therefore be read as approximate, and the pattern is noted wherever employers are discussed.

Missing-Value Summary

Coverage varies widely. Title, employer, occupation, date, and education are complete. Salary, location, and work arrangement are partial, and each is reported against its own denominator rather than against the full panel. Experience is absent entirely.

Analytical Limitations

Five limitations carry into the rest of the project.

Skill lists are alphabetically truncated. No skill beginning with a letter from D to Z appears anywhere in the source extract. Python, SQL, R, Tableau, and similar tools cannot be observed. This constrains the skill-gap analysis in Step 3 more than any other issue on this page.

Experience requirements are absent. Both experience fields are blank across all 165,386 postings, so the analysis substitutes education where experience would otherwise be reported.

Salary reflects disclosing employers only. Pay appears on 39 percent of postings. Employers that disclose salary may differ systematically from those that do not, so salary figures describe the disclosing subset rather than the market.

The window is one quarter. The extract spans 2026-03-25 to 2026-06-29. Seasonal hiring patterns cannot be observed, and any trend over time within the panel should be treated as noise rather than signal.

The panel is a sample, not a census. 287 postings in one industry over one quarter is enough to describe patterns, not enough to estimate market size. Findings are stated as what these postings show rather than as claims about analytics hiring nationally.

Output

The cleaned panel is written to data/processed/career_market_panel.csv and is committed to the repository so the remaining pages render without access to the raw extract.

To rebuild the panel from source, place the 17 Parquet files in data/MET_CareerCompass_2026/ and run:

python3 scripts/build_panel.py
